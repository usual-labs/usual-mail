Usualmail

One app for all your inboxes, each sorted the way that inbox needs, on a server you run yourself.

Usual Mail is a mail app for people with many inboxes. Your own server keeps a synced copy of each mailbox, sorts every mail by what it is, sums it up in a line with your own AI key, and writes what it works out back to your mailbox. Gmail first, with a Mac app.

What it does today

Your inboxes, as they are

Connect the mailboxes you already have, Gmail first. No new address. Your mailbox stays the main copy of your mail; your server keeps in step with it and writes what it works out back as labels.

Sorted by what it is

Your server’s AI, using your own AI key, tells conversations from newsletters, invoices, receipts, notifications and one-time codes, and sums each up in a line. Home holds the conversations that need you.

First-time senders wait

Mail from someone you haven’t let in waits in Screening until you decide: let them in, or block them.

Nothing behind your back

Every action is recorded with who did it and can be undone. Correct a category and that inbox learns a rule.

Your server, your AI key

You run the server, on your own computer, and you hold the AI key: Vercel’s AI Gateway, OpenAI or Anthropic. Before you save it, you see exactly what is sent and to whom.

Pictures without trackers

Pictures in mail load through your server, with known trackers and invisible pictures taken out first, and only from senders you’ve let in.

How Usual Mail uses your Gmail

You connect Gmail by signing in with Google. Usual Mail asks for one permission, gmail.modify, which lets it read, compose, send and label your mail. It can’t delete mail for good, and it asks for nothing else in your Google Account.

Your mail goes to your own Usual Mail server, never to us. If you add an AI key, parts of each mail go to the AI service you chose, so it can sort the mail and sum it up; you see exactly which parts before you save the key.

  • Reads your messages and labels, to show them in your apps and keep your server’s copy in step.
  • Writes labels under “Usual Mail/” for each kind of mail, moves mail in and out of your inbox, marks it read or unread, and moves it to the trash when you ask.
  • Sends only the mail you write and choose to send.
  • Stops when you remove the inbox, which also asks Google to revoke its access, or when you remove Usual Mail’s access in your Google Account.

Where it stands

Usual Mail is early and in development. The first build is in progress and nothing is released, so there is nothing to download yet.

Today it runs on a server you set up on a computer of your own, with Gmail connected by signing in with Google and a Mac app paired to it. IMAP is built but not yet ready for real mail; Outlook and an iPhone app come later.
